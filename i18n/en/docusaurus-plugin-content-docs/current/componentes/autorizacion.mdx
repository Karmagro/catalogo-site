## Description {/* #descripcion */}

A component that determines which resources and operations an authenticated entity can use within the system, based on its identity, role, or attributes. Its purpose is to answer the question "what can this user do?" once their identity has already been verified. It operates autonomously and across the whole system: every time an entity attempts to access a resource or perform an operation, the system silently evaluates whether it has the necessary permissions and grants or denies access according to the defined policies.

- **Administrative management of roles and permissions:** lets authorized administrators create, modify, and assign roles and permissions to users through a specialized interface
- **Fine-grained control:** controls access not only to operations but also to specific entities or to individual attributes within an entity
- **Evaluation caching:** temporarily stores access decisions to reduce the system load of repeated evaluations
- **Auditing of access decisions:** records which users accessed which resources and when, complementing the general [Audit Log](/docs/componentes/log-auditoria)

</div>

## Boundaries {/* #delimitaciones */}

<Admonition type="danger" title="What it is not">

- **Is not** [Authentication](/docs/componentes/formulario-login): authentication verifies who the entity is; authorization determines what it can do once authenticated
- **Does not** intrinsically include the creation or modification of roles or permissions; when that functionality exists, it takes the form of a specialized [Data Manager](/docs/componentes/mantenedor)
- **Is not** visible to the end user as a functionality of its own: it operates in the background, restricting or enabling access to other components of the system

</Admonition>

## Variants {/* #modalidades */}

- **Role-based access control (RBAC):** permissions are assigned to predefined roles (administrator, editor, reader), and users inherit the permissions of the role assigned to them. It is the most common model in information systems.
- **Discretionary access control (DAC):** the owner of a resource determines who can access it and with what level of permissions. Common in document management systems and cloud storage.
- **Attribute-based access control (ABAC):** permissions are evaluated dynamically based on attributes of the user, the resource, and the context (time of day, location). It allows more granular and flexible policies.
